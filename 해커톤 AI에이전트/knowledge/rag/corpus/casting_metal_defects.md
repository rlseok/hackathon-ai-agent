# Gas porosity
- Looks like: bubbles (pores) on the surface or trapped inside; large ones are blowholes/blisters. Typical pore size 0.01–0.5 mm (up to ~1 mm in large castings). Locally reduces strength.
- Causes: dissolved gas (N2, O2, H2) leaves solution during solidification; H2 from metal reacting with mould moisture; turbulent pouring entraining air; steam/smoke from sand; grease or residue in the mould.
- Detection: blowholes can have a sound skin, so use ultrasonic, magnetic-particle, X-ray or industrial CT. Gas porosity vs micro-shrinkage can be hard to tell apart.
- Prevent / correct: melt under vacuum, low-solubility gas (argon, CO2) or protective flux; low superheat; streamlined moulds to cut turbulence; vacuum degassing / gas flushing; dry the mould.

# Shrinkage defects
- Looks like: jagged or linear voids; open shrinkage reaches the surface (pipes, caved surfaces), closed shrinkage is inside. Macro-porosity is visible, micro-porosity is not.
- Causes: not enough feed metal as thick sections solidify; isolated hot spots; impurities/dissolved gas giving nucleation points; wide solidification range alloys without proper risers.
- Prevent / correct: proper risering and feeding, change alloy chemistry or cooling practice, minimise impurities and dissolved gas.

# Misrun and cold shut
- Misrun: cavity not completely filled. Cold shut: weak line where two metal fronts failed to fuse.
- Causes: low metal fluidity, narrow sections, back pressure from poorly vented cavities; in die casting also cold dies, low metal temperature, dirty metal, too much lubricant.
- Prevent / correct: raise fluidity (composition, pouring temperature within limits), improve venting, avoid thin sections, heat dies.

# Inclusions
- Looks like: solid (dross) or liquid (slag) contamination inside the metal. Causes: oxides/nitrides/carbides/sulfides in the melt, eroded furnace or ladle lining, mould contamination.
- Prevent: flux or inert/vacuum melting, skim dross, bottom-pour ladles, ceramic filters in the gating, swirl gates.

# Hot tears (hot cracking) and hot spots
- Hot tears: cracks that form during cooling because the metal is weak when hot and residual stress is high. Hot spots: sections cooling slower than the surroundings, leading to porosity and cracks.
- Prevent: proper mould design, avoid overheating, raise mould temperature, control cooling or change composition.

# Sand-casting surface defects
- Mould erosion (rough spots, embedded sand): weak sand or too-fast pouring -> stronger sand, larger runners / multiple gates.
- Metal penetration (rough surface): coarse sand, no mould wash, high pouring temperature -> finer sand, mould wash, lower pouring temperature.
- Sand fusion (brittle glassy surface): too high pouring temperature or low-melting sand.
- Scabs, buckles, rattails, pulldowns: visual surface defects from high pouring temperature or too little carbonaceous additive; usually not a reason to scrap.
- Burn-on (sand particles embedded in surface): metal oxides reacting with silica-sand impurities -> lower metal temperature, mould wash, additives.
- Run out (metal leaks from mould): faulty mould or flask. Drops / swell: improperly rammed mould.
